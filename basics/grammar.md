# Basic Grammar

## 1. Asking "What?": なに / なん (Nani / Nan)

This is the Japanese equivalent of "what." The choice between なに and なん depends on the sound that follows it.

- なに (nani): Used when it stands alone, or before sounds in the 't', 'd', or 'n' columns.

        これは なに ですか？ (Kore wa nani desu ka?) - What is this?
        あなたの名前は なに ですか？ (Anata no namae wa nani desu ka?) - What is your name?
            なまえ (namae) = name

- なん (nan): Used when followed by counter words (which we'll learn later) or certain particles like で (de) or と (to). You'll encounter this more naturally as you progress.

        なんじ ですか？ (Nanji desu ka?) - What time is it?
            じ (ji) = time (suffix)


## 2. Introduction to Japanese Verbs: ます (masu) Form

Japanese verbs are usually very regular and easy to conjugate in their basic forms.
The polite present/future affirmative form ends in ます (masu).
The verb always comes at the end of the sentence.

Let's learn a couple of fundamental verbs:

    たべます (tabemasu) - to eat
        Originates from たべる (taberu), polite form is たべます.

    のみます (nomimasu) - to drink
        Originates from のむ (nomu), polite form is のみます.


### Basic Sentence Structure with Verbs:[Noun] を (o) [Verb] ます (masu).

- `を (o)`: This is the direct object particle. It marks the noun that the verb's action is performed on. Even though it's written を (wo), it is always pronounced お (o) when used as a particle. This is one of the most common particles you'll encounter.

Examples:

    パン を たべます。 (Pan o tabemasu.) - I eat bread. / (I) will eat bread.
        パン (pan) is Katakana for "bread" (from Portuguese 'pão').
        (パン オ たべます) - Note that the subject "I" is often omitted if it's clear from context.

    水 を のみます。 (Mizu o nomimasu.) - I drink water. / (I) will drink water.
        みず (mizu) = water.

    コーヒー を のみます。 (Kōhī o nomimasu.) - I drink coffee. / (I) will drink coffee.
        コーヒー (kōhī) is Katakana for "coffee."


## 3. Negative Form: ません (masen)

To make a verb negative (do not/will not), you change ます (masu) to ません (masen).

`[Noun] を (o) [Verb] ません (masen).`

Examples:

    パン を たべません。 (Pan o tabemasen.) - I do not eat bread. / (I) will not eat bread.
    水 を のみません。 (Mizu o nomimasen.) - I do not drink water. / (I) will not drink water.


## 4. Asking Questions with Verbs:

Just like with です, add か (ka) to the end of a verb sentence to make it a question.

`[Noun] を (o) [Verb] ます (masu) か (ka)?`

Examples:

    パン を たべますか？ (Pan o tabemasu ka?) - Do you eat bread? / Will you eat bread?
    水 を のみますか？ (Mizu o nomimasu ka?) - Do you drink water? / Will you drink water?

Possible Answers:

    はい、たべます。(Hai, tabemasu.) - Yes, I eat (it). / Yes, I will eat (it).
    いいえ、たべません。(Iie, tabemasen.) - No, I don't eat (it). / No, I won't eat (it).
